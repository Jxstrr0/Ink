# Ink: the game as built

This file describes what is actually in the game today (the plan for what comes next is `docs/INK-PLAN.md`). Update it in the same PR as the feature.

## 0.1.25.0: green leaves

Kelbo's ask: leaves in their own colour. An element can now have a second part (`fill2`) with a fixed colour (`colour2`) that the player doesn't pick.

- **Which motifs.** The traditional rose, cherries, peony, chrysanthemum, cherry blossom and olive branch have green leaves (the rose's leaves are split out of its fill; the chrysanthemum and peony gain two leaves each, the cherry blossom a stem and two leaves; the olive branch's leaves are its second part and the olives stay its main fill). Every element's box is unchanged, so old layouts don't move. The oak leaf, fern, wheat, pine and the game-icons silhouettes (bold rose, daisy, bold lotus, mushroom) have no clean split and stay as they were.
- **The black and grey rule.** The leaves are green only on a coloured element. A black or grey element (no colour, black or grey) keeps its leaves in the same ink. A style with no green in its palette (fine line, blackwork, lettering, geometric) inks the leaves black (`SIM.fill2Colour`).
- **The sim counts them** at their share of the filled area: contrast on the skin and the pale-colour stress line, a "green" must-have, the colour share behind hours and price, green's fade rate as the piece ages. In the chair the leaves are their own zone, labelled "Leaves", after the main zones; on a black rose they are part of the one shading zone. The sailor's rose flash is now a red zone of 1271 mm², the banner's 1981 and the leaves' 251 (was red 1529 and banner 1981). Kit inks are unchanged (they are quality tiers, not colours).
- **Drawn green everywhere:** the design board and the element drawer, the cover-up's old piece (black, by the rule), the flash walls and your own flash, the body (atlas, close-up tiles, shading, halo, aging, gloss), the session canvas, the gallery, the reveal and share cards. The design board stays cream with black lines.
- No new S fields, no new localStorage keys; the design element shape is unchanged.

## 0.1.24.0: a longer aim, clearer collectors bar, a clean end to a sitting

Play-test fixes from Kelbo. The sim and its scores are unchanged.

- **A longer aim.** The hold before the machine starts in the chair is now 1.2 seconds (`SE_ARM.ms` 1200, was 900), and 0.6 seconds when you carry on a line or zone you've already started (`SE_ARM.rearm` 600, was 450). The switch pulse is the same.
- **The collectors bar names both numbers.** Under the collectors bar on the Career tab it now reads "At 60: a waiting list. At 90: your own legacy studio (or wait for 2018)" instead of saying Act 4 opens at 60. Since 0.1.20.0 the shortcut to Act 4 is collectors 90 (`LEGACY_NEED.collectors`); the waiting_list number stays 60 and the 2018 date is unchanged. All three numbers are read from the tables; past 60 the bar names the next thing, and the 90 shortcut until it is reached, 2018 comes or Act 4 opens.
- **No error at the end of a sitting.** Finishing the last line or zone moved the close-up camera, and its redraw came about a quarter of a second later, after the results had already cleared the sitting: a page error ("Cannot read properties of null (reading 'design')") on every finished sitting. The chair now stops everything it scheduled (the camera move, the aim timer, the End and Restart confirmations, the frame loop, the resize watcher) when it closes (`seHalt` in `app/session.js`), and the redraw checks there is still a sitting.
- **Save to booking moves the Next line on.** On the Design tab, saving a design to a booking now changes the Next line to "… is in the chair this week and the design is ready" with its Start button at once, instead of still saying "Press Save to booking".
- No new S fields, no new localStorage keys.

## 0.1.23.0: the shop sounds like a shop

Ambience, sound effects and a shop radio, all made by Kelbo with ElevenLabs. The sim is unchanged.

- **The room.** Each room has its own quiet bed of sound under everything (`AMB_ROOMS` in `sound/synth.js`): Arkady's back room (and his front room, for the chair at Sedov's) is room tone with a machine far off; the rented chair across town is a busy shop, several machines and murmur; your studio is quiet; the legacy studio is busier. When the room changes it crossfades over about a second and a half. In the wet weeks of the year (spring thaw, weeks 14 to 17, and autumn, weeks 37 to 46: `AMB.rain`) rain on the window is layered in quietly; the game has no weather, and the shop window always shows the canal at night. The room and the radio duck under the machine buzz while the needle is going.
- **Sound effects** at the real moments (`SFX`): gloves snapped on when a sitting starts, the stencil paper peeled off when you start after placing it, a spray-and-wipe on a break, an ink cap when a colour zone opens or you use or buy an ink, the shop door when Next brings a walk-in, a return or a referral, the machine set down on the tray when a sitting ends and the till when it paid, a sheet of paper when you save flash, a whoosh into a guitar chord when a bandage comes off on the Career tab, and a short guitar sting instead of the chime when a goal is reached. Each one plays at most once every 300 ms; until a sound has loaded, the old tap plays instead.
- **The radio.** Two instrumental tracks, laid-back surf rockabilly and late-night lo-fi, played in turn, quieter than the room. Off by default.
- **Settings › Sound** has Sound (everything), Ambience (on by default) and Radio (off by default). Sound off silences all of it.
- **Loading.** Nothing loads before your first tap. Then only the current room's 24-second loop (about 140 KB) is fetched; the effects (about 140 KB in all) load when the phone is idle; the radio streams through one audio element (no whole-track decode) and is only fetched once you turn it on. Nothing is added to the offline precache; the service worker keeps what it fetches. A hidden page goes quiet and comes back when shown.
- Two new settings, `S.settings.ambience` and `S.settings.radio`. No new localStorage keys.

## 0.1.22.0: the machine keeps going, and Restart

Kelbo: "the starting isn't working right when live tattooing, it's coming on for a second then stopping leaving dots". The sim's scoring is unchanged.

- **Why it stopped.** Since 0.1.21.0 you hold still for about a second before the machine starts. On a phone, a finger held still that long is a long press: Chrome on Android fires its context menu and drag after about half a second, and iOS Safari its callout, loupe or haptic touch. Nothing in the chair told the browser not to, so as soon as the finger moved after the machine came on, the phone took the touch over and sent the page a cancel. The game read that cancel as a lift: the line ended at a dot, the buzz stopped and the pen vanished.
- **The fix.** The chair now cancels the browser's own touch handling on the canvas (touchstart and touchmove, so no long press, callout, loupe or text selection) and blocks the context menu, text selection and dragging over the chair, with `-webkit-touch-callout:none` on it. If the phone still takes a touch, the line under the canvas says so ("The phone took the touch. Carry on from the dot.") instead of pretending you lifted, and a pointer capture lost while the finger is still down is taken back. Touch times now come from the event only when they are on the same clock as the page (`seEvT`); a move that arrives from before the machine started is no longer drawn as ink the sim never scored; and a zone's aim message gives way to "Swipe back and forth inside the zone" once it starts.
- **Restart.** A **Restart** button on the chair's side rail (practice: above End; with a client: between Chat and End) puts the sitting back to how it began: today's ink, the line and zone progress, pain, fatigue, the clock and any numbing cream bought this sitting (refunded). Earlier sittings of a big piece stay. It asks first the way End does: tap once and it turns red and says **Sure?** with a toast; tap again within a few seconds to wipe the sitting. It is greyed out while the needle is down or aiming, and until something has changed. It only exists before the results, so money, XP and results are counted once. The copy it restores is taken when the work starts (`base` inside the saved sitting), so it survives a reload; a sitting saved by an older version has no copy and no button.
- No new localStorage keys. One new saved value: `S.session.live.base` (the restart copy).

## 0.1.21.0: aim, then the machine starts

Kelbo asked for a moment to see where you're starting before the needle goes in. App only: the sim, its scores and the session traces are unchanged.

- **Aim first.** A finger on the skin now aims instead of inking. For about a second (`SE_ARM.ms` 900 ms in `app/session.js`; 1200 ms since 0.1.24.0) the machine follows your finger as a hollow dashed outline, its tip ring dashed, while a teal ring fills round the needle tip. Nothing is laid, the buzz is quiet and the line's pace marker waits; the start dot still lights up when the tip is near it, so you can line it up. With **Needle under finger** a dashed crosshair reticle at your fingertip does the same job.
- **Then it starts.** When the ring is full the machine switches on: a short teal pulse, the solid pen, the buzz spinning up from a low hum (`BUZZ.spin`, about 0.16 s), and the line starts from where the tip is (if it's already on the dot) or the moment you slide it there, exactly as before. Zones start inking where you are.
- **Carrying on is quicker.** Lift mid-line (or mid-zone) and touch again: the aim is half as long (`SE_ARM.rearm` 450 ms; 600 ms since 0.1.24.0).
- **A quick tap inks nothing.** Lift before the ring fills and nothing happens to the skin; the line under the canvas says "Hold still a moment: the machine starts after a beat." A two-finger pinch during the aim still switches the view and cancels the aim.
- **Reduced motion.** The ring is a static dashed circle with no fill and no pulse; the wait is the same.
- No new S fields, no new localStorage keys, no new setting.

## 0.1.20.0: a fairer climb

Kelbo's difficulty package, from thirty-year careers played through the real sim (careful, average and Skip-the-broom players, 20 careers each). Before, prices climbed so fast with reputation that most clients walked away at the quote, the Drawbridge Prize and conventions were won almost every time, and the studio and Act 4 came very early.

- **Prices.** Your hourly rate still starts from the same base for each act, but rises less per point of reputation (`RATE_BY_REP.perRep`: walk-ins 1 a point, was 2; collectors 3, was 6; peers 1.5, was 3). In the audit about 36% of quotes are accepted instead of 17–21%, and your chair, studio and legacy years are busier.
- **Honours are harder.** The Drawbridge Prize places need a judged score of 0.78 / 0.72 / 0.66 (was 0.66 / 0.58 / 0.5): a careful player wins about one entry in four instead of nearly all of them. Conventions need 0.9 / 0.84 / 0.78 (was 0.72 / 0.62 / 0.52).
- **The studio waits a little longer.** Leasing before 2006 needs walk-ins + collectors + peers of 200 (was 90); from 2006 it is open as before.
- **Act 4 has its own gate.** The waiting list opens from 2018 as before, or sooner once collectors reach 90 (`LEGACY_NEED.collectors`; it used to borrow the waiting_list event's 60, which stays as it is).
- **Leaving Arkady early** costs more of his goodwill for each year left before 2003 (`MENTOR.goodwill.earlyPerYear` -0.30, was -0.18).
- **Inspections.** A passed inspection now clears one fail from your record, so one bad year doesn't hang over the shop forever.
- **Smaller saves.** Older pieces are saved as one short line of numbers each (the same data, nothing rounded further), so a thirty-year save with these busier careers still fits: the test career's year-30 save is about 1.6 MB with a backup code of about 1.0 MB. Saves and codes from 0.1.19.0 load as they are and are written in the new form on the next save.

## 0.1.17.0: tattoos that look like tattoos

How a piece looks on the 3D body (`vis/decals.js`, tuning in `DC_LOOK`, per-tier sizes in `TIER` in `vis/core.js`). Only the picture changed: the sim's scores, heals and aging numbers are the same, and the design board, the flash wall, the gallery, the share cards and the chair's laid ink are untouched.

- **Bolder linework.** Lines are drawn at the sim's line width times 1.3 with a soft bleed of their own ink under them, so they read at real size and the edges are crisp but not hairline. Aging still widens and softens them (`INK_SIM.age`).
- **Packed colour and shading.** A fill reads nearly solid (a fill of 0.7 shows at about 93%), with soft black shading inside it along its outline; watercolour fills get a soft edge instead. Colour only goes where the design has it (the element's own fill), so the body matches the board and the session's colour zones. The traditional motifs (rose, swallow, heart, dagger, anchor, koi, peony, gem, flame and the rest) fill their whole shape, so a sailor's rose or a swallow comes out solid colour inside black lines; fineline motifs, script, frames and textures stay line-only. Ink now shows a share of its own colour over the skin, so a blue or green still reads on warm or dark skin; pale colours still sink into dark skin.
- **Fresh and healed.** A piece that hasn't healed yet has a faint red halo round the ink and a wet sheen; both settle when it heals (two weeks), a little sheen lingers to about ten weeks, and an infected or blown-out heal keeps some redness for a while. Old black drifts blue-grey as it fades.
- **Sharper up close.** The skin texture only gives an arm about 0.75 pixels per millimetre on Lite (1.5 on Full and Ultra), and its layout can't change because the mannequin shares it. So each piece also gets its own tile in a second texture at up to 7 px/mm on Lite, 10 on Full and 12 on Ultra, and the skin shader reads the tile wherever the piece is. The close-up, reveal and placement views are now sharp. Up to 8 tiles on Lite and 16 on Full and Ultra; more pieces share the space at a lower density, and anything left over still shows from the main texture.
- **Memory.** Lite: skin 1024 + tiles 1024 + gloss 512, about 12 MB of GPU memory (5 MB before). Full and Ultra: 2048 + 2048 + 1024, about 48 MB (21 MB before). No 4096 textures: one would cost about 85 MB on a phone.
- Repaints still happen only when a client's pieces, shape or age change, never per frame. No new S fields, no new localStorage keys.

## 0.1.16.0: hold the machine, see the line, know what's next

- **The machine in your hand.** In the chair you now hold the machine: touch anywhere on the skin and it appears, drawn to match your kit (the coil, the rotary or the wireless pen), with its needle tip about 70 px above and a little to the side of your finger, leaning towards your free hand (Settings › Session › Your hand). Your finger never covers the line. Bring the tip onto the start dot and the line starts; everything the sim hears (steady, on the line, pace, depth, joins, blowouts, zones) is the needle tip, not your finger. A ring and a glow show when the tip is near the dot. Lift and the machine goes away. Two fingers on the skin pinch the view; a palm resting on the screen is ignored.
- **Needle under finger** (Settings › Session) turns the offset off and puts the needle back under your fingertip, as before.
- **Close up.** The chair zooms onto the part you're working on: each line, shading zone or colour zone is framed big in the view (up to three times), and the camera glides to the next part when one is done. The **Whole** button (top corner, on your free hand's side) shows the whole piece; **Close up** goes back. The ink you've laid stays put through every move. Reduced motion skips the glide.
- **Bigger targets.** Every button, chip, tab and toggle is at least 44 by 44 px; the session rail and the liner chips are bigger; nothing scrolls sideways at 360 px.
- **What to do now.** Every tab has one line at the top saying the one thing to do next, with a button that does it: back to a sitting you left, see your Legacy, back to a consult, see a heal, a choice card to decide, start a sitting whose design is ready, design a booked sitting, talk to someone at the door, the sample piece's next sitting, Act 1's step (a chore, practice, a flash sheet, a friend's free piece, your first walk-in), and otherwise Next week. Where the control is already on screen it is just the line. It hides in the chair and in a consult. The Clients tab's "Nobody at the door" card now has a **Next week** button that moves the clock on.
- **An easier design board.** Selecting an element shows a bigger corner dot (drag to size and turn, as before) and a separate turn dot on a stalk above it; each element has a larger grab area. Undo, Redo, Copy, Forward and Delete have words under their icons. Two fingers with nothing selected pick the element between them and pinch it. Snap to flow, undo and redo work as before.
- No new localStorage keys. New S field: `settings.needle` (`"offset"` or `"direct"`).

## 0.1.13.0: days and honours, quieter toasts, names on the cards

- **Flash days.** Once walk-in reputation reaches 40 and you have at least three sheets of your own flash on the wall, a choice card now and then (never twice within half a year) offers a flash day: small pieces off your wall at one fixed price, first come, first served. Run it and the day is a stream of quick sittings (about 6 to 18, more with reputation): money in the till after supplies, walk-in and peer reputation (more with a full wall of your own sheets) and a little speed and linework. Leave it and nothing happens.
- **Friday the 13th.** In a week that really holds a Friday the 13th (one to three a year, the real dates), with walk-in reputation at 60, the card offers the old Friday-13th flash special: a bigger day than a flash day, cheaper pieces, more walk-in reputation. It has to be taken that week.
- **A feature in Kontur.** Kontur is Mostograd's tattoo quarterly (invented). With collectors at 65 and at least four healed portfolio pieces that still read well, it may ask to feature you, leading with your best one; at most three features in a career, years apart. Yes raises collectors most (and walk-ins and peers a little) and sends a collector who read the piece; no is a small nod from peers.
- **The Drawbridge Prize.** Every year in week 48, with peers at 75 and at least two healed pieces a year old or more, the Mostograd Tattoo Guild puts you up for its prize. It's judged on your three best healed pieces (how they read today, plus a little for your signature style): first, second or third place pays a prize and raises peers and collectors; no placing still earns a little respect. Entering is the default.
- **Days and honours.** A card on the Career tab lists the flash days and Friday specials you've run (and how many pieces), the years Kontur featured you and your Drawbridge placings. The reputation bars now name what each track really opens next (flash days, the Friday special, the waiting list, Kontur, guest spots, the capital's convention, the prize). The new cards have a teal edge so they read apart from the rivals'.
- **Quieter toasts.** The chime now plays only for toasts that matter: a goal reached, an event card arriving, a bandage coming off, a finished piece, a big step (your own chair, the lease, Act 4, a hire, a convention placing, the TV call) and a warning (storage full). Confirmations and hints show silently.
- **Names on the share cards.** A piece card now says whose it is: "Anton's left forearm · Traditional · 2000". Every client is invented; the credit line stays.
- **Tips and Skip the broom.** A career started with Skip the broom starts with tips off (it's for players who know the ropes); Settings › Help turns them on. Starting as an apprentice turns them on.

## 0.1.6.0: the 3D body and the shop

No face, no nudity: a strapless bandeau (front panel only, so the chest, ribs and the whole back stay bare for work) and shorts. Three body shapes (neutral, masc, femme) share one mesh with identical UVs; a shape change blends between them over about half a second (instant with reduced motion), and pieces stay exactly where they are. Girth follows the `BODY_SHAPES` wrap table. Skin colour is the same palette as the body diagram (`bmSkin`). About 6,600 triangles.
- **One UV island per zone:** left and right arm, hand, leg and foot, chest, back, left and right side, neck (plus one blank island for clothes and the thumbs). Islands are unwrapped in true millimetres of the neutral body, so a placement's box in mm (`placementBox`) maps straight onto the skin; each placement sits on its island by an anchor (the elbow crease, the knee, the armpit, the top of the shoulder, ...).
- **Tattoos on the skin (`vis/decals.js`):** each piece is drawn with the design board's own element paths into its island, as it looks today: the aging model (`INK_SIM.age`, with the piece's heal from PR 5, its ink and the client's sun) gives the blur, spread and fade. One skin texture per client (1024 on Lite, 2048 on Full and Ultra), redrawn only when the pieces or the shape change; a piece never spills off its island. The stencil is a separate purple layer on top.
- **The shop (`vis/studio.js`):** walls, plank floor, the tattoo chair with armrests that follow the client's forearms, your station (trolley, coil machine and, since 0.1.12.0, a rotary pen, ink caps, paper roll), a swing-arm lamp, the stool, shelves, a door and a bench, and a window onto the canal at night (lit facades and a raised drawbridge). About 3,000 triangles; no shadows on Lite, lamp shadows on Full and Ultra.
- **Rendering:** only when something changes. A frame loop runs during a camera move, a shape change or a drag (30 fps cap on Lite), and `INK_VIS.fps()` is null when still, so auto-adjust only judges real motion. Camera presets: consult (face-on), stencil and session (close on the zone, from the side that keeps the chair out of the way), reveal; moves ease over up to a second, instant with reduced motion. Drag the view to look round (less on Lite).
- **Studio tab:** the 3D shop on top. Who's in the chair: the client of a sitting in progress, else the next booked client, else your last client, else the design board's client, with their pieces on them as they look today and chips for Face-on, The work and Reveal. ## 0.1.5.0: heal, reveal, body of work

- **Healing:** two weeks after a piece's last sitting the bandage comes off (Career › Next stops for it). The heal grade comes from the session: lines, depth, saturation and trauma, the joins, the blowouts, and the client's aftercare. It is **crisp**, **slightly soft**, **patchy**, **blown out** (the more blowouts, the likelier) or **infected** (rare: only when trauma was high *and* the client's aftercare is poor; a gentle sitting never gets infected). The grade stays with the piece and changes how it ages from then on (a patchy or blown-out heal starts the aging model with spread and fade already applied).
- **What a heal does:** the client remembers it (a heal line in their history) and their loyalty moves (up for crisp, down for patchy, blown out, infected). Some write a review (stars and a line; unhappy clients are likelier to). Referrals only come from clean heals. An **infection** costs reputation and books a **free fix** three weeks later (a price-0 sitting, "free fix after an infection" on the Clients tab).
- **The reveal (Career tab):** each healed piece waits at the top of the Career tab. A verdict like PEW's analysis screen: the grade as a shape and a word (● crisp, ◐ slightly soft, dotted ◌ patchy, ✱ blown out, ▲ infected) and how well it reads today. The piece the day you did it next to how it healed, with your stencil laid over it as a faint dashed purple ghost, so spread and blowouts show against where the lines were meant to be (a gauze pad peels off the healed side; not with reduced motion). The client's reaction (by grade and how loyal they are), the review if they wrote one, and for an infection an apology scene. Then **Put it in your portfolio?** Add it or not; one tap moves to the next reveal.
- **Reputation:** three tracks, 0 to 100, on the Career card with what each opens next. **Walk-ins** (speed and price: clean heals, flash, small pieces, good reviews), **collectors** (custom work and style: custom pieces, bigger ones, styles you've mastered, the portfolio), **peers** (clean heals; blowouts and infections hurt most). Gains slow as a track fills and every track slips a little each week. Each track pulls in its kinds of walk-in only once it reaches their number (students at walk-ins 15, collectors at collectors 25, cover-ups at peers 35, ...); the events they open (flash days, the Friday 13th special, the waiting list, a feature in Kontur, guest spots, the big convention, the Drawbridge Prize) are all in the game.
- **Body of work (Clients › Work):** every piece you have finished, newest first, drawn on the client's skin as it looks *today*: lines spread, blur and fade with the years through the aging model. Filter by year, style, client name and portfolio (★). 40 at a time with Show more, so a long career stays quick. Tap a piece for its history: the date and stage, the session outputs, the kit, the heal, the review, how it looks healed, at 5 and 10 years and today, a portfolio button, and the client's ledger (their pieces and what they've paid) with a link to their card. Tapping a piece on a client's card opens the same history.
- Sample walk-ins (Tattoo this on the Design tab) heal too, with their own aftercare; practice on fake skin never makes a piece, so it never heals.

## 0.1.4.0: the session and the kit

- **Session tab:** who's in the chair this week (booked sittings with a design, started from here or from the Clients tab's Start session), a sample walk-in's unfinished piece, and Practice on fake skin (the board's design or any flash piece). With nobody booked: "No one in the chair. Book a client from the Clients tab." and a Practice button.
- **Tattoo this** under the design board starts the booking the board is designing for (it saves the design to the booking first, and only in the booking's week), or else a one-off sitting on the sample walk-in. **Practice on fake skin** sits next to it.
- **Placing the stencil:** the purple stencil on the placement with its flow lines and creases; drag it, and flow and crease problems are checked again.
- **Linework (trace-and-rhythm):** start on the dot and follow each stencil line. A marker runs along it at the target pace (with reduced motion, a fixed highlight of the next stretch instead). The steady-hand gauge shows your pace against the band: slow, steady (green), rushed (hatched). Each line is scored per segment for how close you stay and how steady your pace is; rushing past the band blows the line out (it spreads), lifting or stalling and starting again leaves a join, and lingering digs in (trauma). The liner matters: each needle lays its own weight and the one either side ("lays 5–9RL"); a line the liner can't lay costs quality, and the chips say which one to switch to.
- **Shading and colour:** hold and sweep inside a shading zone, or swipe back and forth in a colour zone (colour only packs while you keep reversing). The meter fills to a line; past the hatching you are overworking the skin and it goes red. Next zone moves on.
- **The client:** pain (placement, their tolerance, the machine and voltage, time in the chair, any trauma) and fatigue rise as you work; both meters have notches, a pattern and an icon, and a warning triangle near the top. Break (less each time), Numb (numbing cream, 250 ₽ in 2000 money, takes the edge off for two hours) and Chat (helps with a talkative client, wears on a quiet one). Either meter full ends the sitting. Handedness (Settings › Session) puts these buttons on your free hand's side.
- **Sittings and pieces:** a big piece is split into sittings by hours, mostly linework first. A booked sitting pays its share of the balance and books the next one; progress rides on the booking. Finish everything early and the balance is paid then. If pain or fatigue stops the last sitting, the client comes back the next week to finish, and pays then. The last sitting makes the piece, with its outputs (lines, depth, saturation, trauma, sittings, blowouts, joins), the ink and machine, and the heal due two weeks later (shown in PR 5). Sample walk-ins pay you 35% of the price (the shop keeps the rest) and can sit again straight away. Practice pays nothing and makes no piece. Every sitting gives XP in linework, shading, colour, speed and the style.
- **A sitting survives a reload:** it's saved at every finger lift and comes back exactly where it was. Career › Next waits while a booked sitting is in the chair.
- **The kit (Studio tab):** a summary (steady band, the line weights the tray lays, shading speed, skin trauma, ink fade) and four views: Machine (used coil, coil, rotary, wireless pen from 2014), Needles (liners 1RL to 14RL and mags; up to 5 on the tray), Ink (budget, standard, premium), Tune (voltage and stroke sliders). Every option shows ▲/▼ deltas against what you have. A new career starts with 3,000 ₽ and the starter kit: a used coil, 3RL, 7RL and a 7 mag, budget ink.
- **Sound:** a machine buzz under your finger whose pitch follows your pace and whose tone follows the machine (the used coil rattles, the pen hums); it stutters when you rush. Off with Settings › Sound.
- **Assist** (Settings › Session) slows the marker and widens what counts as steady and on the line.

## 0.1.3.0: clients, the consult and the week

- **Career tab, Next:** the week clock. Next runs the weeks forward until someone is due in the chair, a walk-in arrives, a piece heals or an event fires, never more than 4 weeks at a time, and shows one line ("Weeks 22 to 25, 2004: …") with the week's log under it: walk-ins who left, returns, referrals, bookings, sittings moved. If anyone is waiting this week the first tap warns ("Skip them? Tap again"): walk-ins leave, sittings move to next week. The career stops at week 52 of 2030.
- **Clients tab, This week:** the chair hours booked against the week's hours and the waiting list; walk-ins at the door (they leave at the end of the week); booked consults (clients coming back for a touch-up, an add-on, a sleeve or a cover-up, always naming their earlier piece and its year, and people sent by a client); the sittings due; what's coming up.
- **The consult:** a short chat (3 to 5 exchanges) with the client's brief, written in Mostograd (the Old Bridge, Lodochnaya Embankment, Kanatnaya Street, the port, the White Nights). Every client is an adult and the consult says "ID checked, 18+". Questions reveal hidden must-haves, dealbreakers, pain tolerance, sun, budget and whether they cover up for work (more often with a better consult skill and a trusting, talkative client). A bad idea (tiny script, a portrait on a hand, fine line on a finger) can be talked out, gently or bluntly: win and the plan changes and they trust you more; lose and they may walk. It ends with a quote (fair, high or low): price, deposit and sittings from the design model; they accept or walk. The deposit goes straight into your cash. Choices replay the same way if you leave and come back.
- **Designing for a booking:** "Design it" opens the board for that client with their body shape, skin tone, placement and style, and a bar with what they asked for and what you know. The board scores fit against what the consult found out, not what's still hidden. "Save to booking" puts the design on the sitting.
- **Sittings:** (replaced by the session in 0.1.4.0) "Quick session" worked a sitting out from your skills. Each sitting pays its share of the balance; the last one makes the piece, named the way the client would say it ("the rose on Darya's left forearm").
- **Client list:** everyone you've consulted, newest first, with filters (All, Regulars, Booked, Walked) and a name search. A client's card shows what you know, a loyalty bar, their pieces as they look today (re-rendered from the design and aged by the model) and their history.
- **Walk-ins** depend on the stage (an apprentice sees one now and then), the season and reputation; returns come from your pieces as they age and from loyalty; referrals arrive a few weeks after a good heal.

## 0.1.2.0: the design board

- **Design tab, three views:** Body, Board and Flash, on a segmented control at the top.
- **Body:** an original front/back drawing of the neutral mannequin (smooth capsule limbs, no face, crop top and shorts so the ribs show), in `app/bodymap.js`. Every one of the 26 placements is a tappable (and keyboard-focusable) zone; picking one shows its room (wrap × length for the body shape), pain, how much it shows and its sun. A body-shape switch (Broad, Slight, Average) reshapes the figure and the wrap widths, and a skin-tone slider tints it; both feed scoring. A list picker does the same for anyone who prefers it.
- **Walk-ins:** four sample clients (Darya, Bogdan, Sasha, Ilya) to score against until real clients arrive in PR 3. Picking one sets the body shape and skin tone.
- **Board:** cream with black ink in both themes. The placement's box at its true aspect in millimetres, with a mm ruler, the flow lines, the joint creases and the piece's footprint (W × H mm). Elements come from a drawer in the sheet, filtered by style, family and tag; locked elements are greyed with what unlocks them, and "Show all (sandbox)" opens everything for testing (not saved). Drag to move, two fingers to pinch-scale and rotate, the corner handle for mouse users, and a toolbar: size, rotate ±15° (90° steps from the flow with snap on), line weight (1 to 14RL), fill, colour swatches from the style's palette (greyed, hatched and labelled "faint" or "won't read" when `SKIN_CONTRAST` says so on this skin tone), duplicate, bring forward, delete. Snap to flow turns an element's long axis along the limb's flow where it sits. Lines are drawn at their true width, so a 1RL line on a big piece is a hairline.
- **Capsule:** docked at the bottom of the board: readability, flow, fit, hours, visits (sessions) and price in rubles, each with a ▲/▼ delta from before the last change (colour and shape). Under it, the piece now and at 5, 10 and 20 years, blurred, faded and with the lines spread by `agePrediction`, with readability under each. The chevron hides the aging row (hidden by default on short phones).
- **Stress report:** every `stress` line with a severity icon (octagon serious, triangle warning, circle note), plus balance, coherence and overall.
- **Flash wall:** 12 pre-composed designs (`FLASH`, `sim/flash.js`) that take 20% fewer hours (`FLASH_DISCOUNT`, `design.flash`). Using one loads it on the current spot (shrunk to fit if needed). Adding or removing elements makes it a custom piece again. "Save to my flash" keeps your own designs (up to 40) as your flash, with the same discount.
- **Undo/redo:** buttons, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y). On desktop, Tab reaches each element; arrows nudge (Shift for 5 mm), + and − size, [ and ] turn, Delete removes, Escape deselects.
- **Saving:** the working design, body shape, skin tone and walk-in (`S.board`) and your flash (`S.flash`) save with the game and ride in backup codes; designs save as parameters, never pictures.
