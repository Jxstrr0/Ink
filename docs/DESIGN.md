# Ink: the game as built

This file describes what is actually in the game today (the plan for what comes next is `docs/INK-PLAN.md`). Update it in the same PR as the feature.

## 0.1.14.0: a room per career stage, your machine on the tray

- **Four different places.** The Studio tab's 3D shop is now a different room at each stage of the career, instead of one room that gains a few props.
  - **Arkady's back room** (apprentice): small and low, dingy green paint with brick showing through under a small high window onto the canal, worn floorboards, cardboard boxes of supplies, cluttered shelves on the back wall, a mop in its bucket, the broom, a practice table with fake skin pads and a practice forearm, Arkady's old flash pinned on a corkboard, and a bare bulb on a cord. His shop, not yours.
  - **The rented chair**: a station in a busy shop with a high ceiling, black-and-cream lino, white tiles to shoulder height and strip lights. The shop's flash fills the wall behind you; a partition stands between you and the next artist, with three of your own sheets taped to it, and their chair and lamp beyond it. The canal window is on their side.
  - **Your studio**: warm plaster over a dark dado, parquet, a round rug, the big canal window, the ИГЛА sign above the chair, your flash wall, the plant under the window, shelves and the waiting bench, a brass pendant.
  - **The established studio** (legacy): bigger, deep green walls over a tall oak wainscot with a brass rail, a patterned rug, the trophy cabinet, framed photos of healed work, two plants, two brass pendants and a second artist's station by the window.
  - Each room has its own light (warm and harsh in the back room, cool and bright under the strip lights, warm in your studios) and the camera stays inside its walls. The chair, the armrests, the client and your trolley, lamp and stool are the same in every room, so the camera views and dragging the stencil on the body work everywhere.
- **Your machine on the tray.** The machine your kit uses (Studio tab › Your kit › Machine) now lies up front on the trolley, and the other one is set aside at the back corner: the coil for the used and new coils, the rotary for the rotary. The wireless pen shows as the rotary for now. It changes as soon as you pick another machine.
- **Lite** keeps the coded rooms (about 2.2k to 3.6k triangles each, only one shows) and loads nothing from `models/`. **Full and Ultra** put the photo textures on every room with its own tint (the rented chair keeps its lino and tiles) and the Blender props follow their stand-ins into each room. The trophy cabinet now has a simple coded stand-in too, so Lite sees it.

## 0.1.13.0: days and honours, quieter toasts, names on the cards

- **Flash days.** Once walk-in reputation reaches 40 and you have at least three sheets of your own flash on the wall, a choice card now and then (never twice within half a year) offers a flash day: small pieces off your wall at one fixed price, first come, first served. Run it and the day is a stream of quick sittings (about 6 to 18, more with reputation): money in the till after supplies, walk-in and peer reputation (more with a full wall of your own sheets) and a little speed and linework. Leave it and nothing happens.
- **Friday the 13th.** In a week that really holds a Friday the 13th (one to three a year, the real dates), with walk-in reputation at 60, the card offers the old Friday-13th flash special: a bigger day than a flash day, cheaper pieces, more walk-in reputation. It has to be taken that week.
- **A feature in Igla.** Igla is Mostograd's tattoo quarterly (invented). With collectors at 75 and at least four healed portfolio pieces that still read well, it may ask to feature you, leading with your best one; at most three features in a career, years apart. Yes raises collectors most (and walk-ins and peers a little) and sends a collector who read the piece; no is a small nod from peers.
- **The Drawbridge Prize.** Every year in week 48, with peers at 85 and at least two healed pieces a year old or more, the Mostograd Tattoo Guild puts you up for its prize. It's judged on your three best healed pieces (how they read today, plus a little for your signature style): first, second or third place pays a prize and raises peers and collectors; no placing still earns a little respect. Entering is the default.
- **Days and honours.** A card on the Career tab lists the flash days and Friday specials you've run (and how many pieces), the years Igla featured you and your Drawbridge placings. The reputation bars now name what each track really opens next (flash days, the Friday special, the waiting list, Igla, guest spots, the capital's convention, the prize). The new cards have a teal edge so they read apart from the rivals'.
- **Quieter toasts.** The chime now plays only for toasts that matter: a goal reached, an event card arriving, a bandage coming off, a finished piece, a big step (your own chair, the lease, Act 4, a hire, a convention placing, the TV call) and a warning (storage full). Confirmations and hints show silently.
- **Names on the share cards.** A piece card now says whose it is: "Anton's left forearm · Traditional · 2000". Every client is invented; the credit line stays.
- **Tips and Skip the broom.** A career started with Skip the broom starts with tips off (it's for players who know the ropes); Settings › Help turns them on. Starting as an apprentice turns them on.

## 0.1.10.0: free assets and the Blender slots (Full and Ultra)

- **A finished-looking shop on Full and Ultra.** The back room gets photo textures from Poly Haven (CC0): a herringbone parquet floor, painted plaster walls (with a little relief from a normal map) and dark planks on the dado, tinted to the room's warm colours. They load after the room is up, a few hundred KB, once, and stay in the offline cache. Lite looks exactly as before and loads none of it.

- **UI sounds.** Taps on tabs and buttons play a soft click from Kenney's Interface Sounds (CC0), and an important toast plays a short chime (0.1.13.0: only goals, events, heals, finished pieces, big steps and warnings); the synth click stays as the fallback and the machine buzz is still synthesised. They load after your first tap (about 30 KB) and follow Settings › Sound.

The page dims with a spotlight on the real control and a card beside it: Got it (or a tap anywhere, or Escape) puts it away for good, No more tips turns them all off. - **Polish.** A phone on its side gets a slim header and a tab bar with the label beside each icon, so more of the screen is the game. Toasts move to the top of the screen while a sheet or a tip is open, so they never cover a sheet's buttons, and they're as wide as they need to be. Goals reached by a convention placing, a lease, a hire or a rival's event tick straight away instead of on the next Next. The reputation bars name only what the game has (guest spots and the big convention; no "later update"). The board's "Add an element" hint, finished Act 1 steps and the light theme's board edge read more clearly.

## 0.1.8.0: events, trends, rivals, social media

- **Trend waves.** Named fashion waves (script on the ribs, the fine-line boom peaking in 2017, a watercolour spike that fades, heavy blackwork, big Japanese work, traditional coming round again) raise or lower what walk-ins ask for, year by year. The Next log says when a wave starts, peaks and fades; the Career tab's **What's in demand** card lists the four most-wanted styles with rising/falling arrows.
- **Social media from 2016.** A **Social media** card on the Career tab: followers drift toward what your reputation earns; **Post a healed piece** (your own work, healed, once per piece; also from a piece's sheet and the reveal) reaches more people for a good design and a crisp heal, and followers gained feed walk-ins and collectors. Followers book from far away. One piece a career can go viral. Clients post bad heals, and that costs followers and reputation.
- **Rival shops.** Four invented Mostograd shops open in their own windows (Seventh Bridge Tattoo by the Old Bridge, the Grey Heron on Lodochnaya Embankment, Atelier Nochnoy on Kanatnaya Street, Konveyer on the outer ring), plus any apprentice who leaves to set up against you. They pull walk-ins away and act: undercut your prices, poach your artists, or copy your flash once you have a signature. Each one waits as a card on the Career tab with choices (match or hold your price, or go and talk; wish an artist well or take a smaller cut to keep them; call out copied flash, let it go, or draw new flash) and a default if you leave it. They remember. They close when your reputation outgrows them.
- **Reckonings,** once per career each: one of your first-year clients walks back in around career years 12 to 18 with your apprentice-era piece, shown then and now (no bandage this time), and you rework it free, cover it (a paid consult) or leave it; the client who bad-mouthed a patchy heal comes back once you're known (fix it free, charge, or turn them away); Arkady sends you a referral in Act 3; the pawn shop on the canal offers to buy an old machine.
- **The inspector** checks the sterilisation room, supplies, budget ink and debt. A fail fines you and can shut the shop for one to three weeks (a banner on the Career tab; walk-ins go elsewhere, booked sittings move).
- **Cover-up puzzles.** Now and then a client brings someone else's old piece. On the board it sits under your work as a locked layer, blurred and blown out the way the aging model says it looks today, with its name under it; the stress report says how much of it your design hides (dark fills and textures hide it, thin lines don't), and the finished score moves the client's loyalty and your peers.
- **Conventions** judge a fresh piece and a healed piece separately (you pick them, or skip a category); booth sales, a collector who books, and other shops' booths.
- **Goals:** ten career goals on the Career tab (first clean heal, first custom piece, first return, first convention award, open the studio, hire an artist, a client with five of your pieces, survive a rival, twenty years and it still reads, an apprentice opens a shop), with a toast when one is reached.
- **Aging:** once lines spread into the gaps, a simple shape still reads as its outline (`READ.shapeHold`), so a tiny old piece reads low instead of 0. Lettering gets no such floor.

## 0.1.7.0: the career, the shop, money and saves

- **Four acts.** Act 1: you're Arkady Sedov's apprentice at Sedov's on Kanatnaya Street. The Career tab's act card gives one goal at a time (a chore, practice on fake skin, a flash sheet, a friend's free piece, a paying walk-in, then your own chair), with Arkady's word on it and his goodwill. Chores each take a week. Walk-ins only reach you once you've done a friend. Leaving before week 1 of 2003 costs his goodwill; with it high he gives you his own chair at a 30% split (else 40%) and will cover you once if your studio sinks. Act 2: a rented chair, your own rate (by act and reputation, times the price index), supplies per booked hour, conventions and guest spots as invitations. Act 3: lease a studio (from 2006, or sooner with reputation), build it out (stations, waiting area, flash wall, sterilisation room, lighting, storefront), pick a vibe and marketing, hire artists, pay rent every week; inspections and no-shows happen. Act 4 (from 2018): close the books to walk-ins (a waiting list), a flash book, a product line, your own convention, apprentices, and the TV offer.
- **Staff** work without the minigame: their pieces come from `simStaffSession`, heal with the same rules, and build the shop's reputation, not yours. They're in a Shop gallery (Clients › Work › Shop), never your body of work. An infection on one of theirs is on the shop. Set each artist's cut; too greedy and they drift off, and other shops poach.
- **Apprentices** you train come out on your staff or across the canal as a rival with your style; their first solo piece is on your most loyal regular.
- **The signature:** after a dozen pieces with one style in about a third of them, people know your hand: some walk-ins ask for "your thing", they pay more for it, your rate rises, and convention judges look for it.
- **Money:** a ledger by kind; the Career tab shows last week in and out. **Going broke** (four weeks below the floor in a studio) closes the shop with a plain scene: staff and the lease go; your clients, every piece and your reputation stay, and you're back in a chair.
- **The Legacy screen:** from the Career tab once you have a studio, and by itself at week 52 of 2030: the oldest piece that still reads, the best heal, who kept coming back, pieces per decade, your signature, conventions, apprentices. The save stays playable.
- **Saves:** three slots in Settings › Save (each squeezed with the backup code's LZW), a New career sheet (the start screen on a first visit) with **Skip the broom** (start in 2003 in a chair). Old clients past 200 are archived to a short record and fill back in if they return; pieces are never dropped. The save is packed (older pieces as short arrays): about 1.5 MB at year 30, its backup code about 0.9 MB. A storage-full save shows a toast and offers a backup code.
- The 3D shop follows the stage (`INK_VIS.setStudio`) on every stage change.

## 0.1.6.0: the 3D body and the shop

- **The mannequin (`vis/body.js`):** a stylized adult figure built in code, seated in the tattoo chair: forearms forward on the armrests, thighs on the seat, shins down, feet flat. No face, no nudity: a strapless bandeau (front panel only, so the chest, ribs and the whole back stay bare for work) and shorts. Three body shapes (neutral, masc, femme) share one mesh with identical UVs; a shape change blends between them over about half a second (instant with reduced motion), and pieces stay exactly where they are. Girth follows the `BODY_SHAPES` wrap table. Skin colour is the same palette as the body diagram (`bmSkin`). About 6,600 triangles.
- **One UV island per zone:** left and right arm, hand, leg and foot, chest, back, left and right side, neck (plus one blank island for clothes and the thumbs). Islands are unwrapped in true millimetres of the neutral body, so a placement's box in mm (`placementBox`) maps straight onto the skin; each placement sits on its island by an anchor (the elbow crease, the knee, the armpit, the top of the shoulder, ...).
- **Tattoos on the skin (`vis/decals.js`):** each piece is drawn with the design board's own element paths into its island, as it looks today: the aging model (`INK_SIM.age`, with the piece's heal from PR 5, its ink and the client's sun) gives the blur, spread and fade. One skin texture per client (1024 on Lite, 2048 on Full and Ultra), redrawn only when the pieces or the shape change; a piece never spills off its island. The stencil is a separate purple layer on top.
- **The shop (`vis/studio.js`):** walls, plank floor, the tattoo chair with armrests that follow the client's forearms, your station (trolley, coil machine and, since 0.1.12.0, a rotary pen, ink caps, paper roll), a swing-arm lamp, the stool, shelves, a door and a bench, and a window onto the canal at night (lit facades and a raised drawbridge). The flash wall fills up as you go. `setStudio(stage)` dresses it for apprentice (boxes and a broom in the corner), chair, studio (a sign, ИГЛА since 0.1.12.0, and a plant) and legacy (framed pieces). About 3,000 triangles; no shadows on Lite, lamp shadows on Full and Ultra.
- **Rendering:** only when something changes. A frame loop runs during a camera move, a shape change or a drag (30 fps cap on Lite), and `INK_VIS.fps()` is null when still, so auto-adjust only judges real motion. Camera presets: consult (face-on), stencil and session (close on the zone, from the side that keeps the chair out of the way), reveal; moves ease over up to a second, instant with reduced motion. Drag the view to look round (less on Lite).
- **Studio tab:** the 3D shop on top. Who's in the chair: the client of a sitting in progress, else the next booked client, else your last client, else the design board's client, with their pieces on them as they look today and chips for Face-on, The work and Reveal. The kit panel (PR 4) is below as before.
- **3D stencil placement (Session tab):** on a client the stencil goes on their body in the chair: drag it on the skin (the touch is raycast to the body's UV and on to placement mm; letting go places it in the sim, which re-checks flow and creases in place), drag off it to look round, and turn it 15 degrees a tap (`INK_SIM.sessionRotate`, which keeps every element's real size). **Flat view** switches to the PR 4 flat box (kept in `S.settings.flatStencil`); fake skin and browsers without WebGL always use the flat box.
- Known limit: in the seated pose the arm hangs over the side, so a ribs piece is partly hidden from most angles (orbit to see it; a raised-arm pose is a later polish item).

## 0.1.5.0: heal, reveal, body of work

- **Healing:** two weeks after a piece's last sitting the bandage comes off (Career › Next stops for it). The heal grade comes from the session: lines, depth, saturation and trauma, the joins, the blowouts, and the client's aftercare. It is **crisp**, **slightly soft**, **patchy**, **blown out** (the more blowouts, the likelier) or **infected** (rare: only when trauma was high *and* the client's aftercare is poor; a gentle sitting never gets infected). The grade stays with the piece and changes how it ages from then on (a patchy or blown-out heal starts the aging model with spread and fade already applied).
- **What a heal does:** the client remembers it (a heal line in their history) and their loyalty moves (up for crisp, down for patchy, blown out, infected). Some write a review (stars and a line; unhappy clients are likelier to). Referrals only come from clean heals. An **infection** costs reputation and books a **free fix** three weeks later (a price-0 sitting, "free fix after an infection" on the Clients tab).
- **The reveal (Career tab):** each healed piece waits at the top of the Career tab. A verdict like PEW's analysis screen: the grade as a shape and a word (● crisp, ◐ slightly soft, dotted ◌ patchy, ✱ blown out, ▲ infected) and how well it reads today. The piece the day you did it next to how it healed, with your stencil laid over it as a faint dashed purple ghost, so spread and blowouts show against where the lines were meant to be (a gauze pad peels off the healed side; not with reduced motion). The client's reaction (by grade and how loyal they are), the review if they wrote one, and for an infection an apology scene. Then **Put it in your portfolio?** Add it or not; one tap moves to the next reveal.
- **Reputation:** three tracks, 0 to 100, on the Career card with what each opens next. **Walk-ins** (speed and price: clean heals, flash, small pieces, good reviews), **collectors** (custom work and style: custom pieces, bigger ones, styles you've mastered, the portfolio), **peers** (clean heals; blowouts and infections hurt most). Gains slow as a track fills and every track slips a little each week. Each track pulls in its kinds of walk-in only once it reaches their number (students at walk-ins 15, collectors at collectors 25, cover-ups at peers 35, ...); the events they open (flash days, the Friday 13th special, the waiting list, a feature in Igla, guest spots, the big convention, the Drawbridge Prize) are all in the game.
- **Body of work (Clients › Work):** every piece you have finished, newest first, drawn on the client's skin as it looks *today*: lines spread, blur and fade with the years through the aging model. Filter by year, style, client name and portfolio (★). 40 at a time with Show more, so a long career stays quick. Tap a piece for its history: the date and stage, the session outputs, the kit, the heal, the review, how it looks healed, at 5 and 10 years and today, a portfolio button, and the client's ledger (their pieces and what they've paid) with a link to their card. Tapping a piece on a client's card opens the same history.
- Sample walk-ins (Tattoo this on the Design tab) heal too, with their own aftercare; practice on fake skin never makes a piece, so it never heals.

## 0.1.4.0: the session and the kit

- **Session tab:** who's in the chair this week (booked sittings with a design, started from here or from the Clients tab's Start session), a sample walk-in's unfinished piece, and Practice on fake skin (the board's design or any flash piece). With nobody booked: "No one in the chair. Book a client from the Clients tab." and a Practice button.
- **Tattoo this** under the design board starts the booking the board is designing for (it saves the design to the booking first, and only in the booking's week), or else a one-off sitting on the sample walk-in. **Practice on fake skin** sits next to it.
- **Placing the stencil:** the purple stencil on the placement with its flow lines and creases; drag it, and flow and crease problems are checked again.
- **Linework (trace-and-rhythm):** start on the dot and follow each stencil line. A marker runs along it at the target pace (with reduced motion, a fixed highlight of the next stretch instead). The steady-hand gauge shows your pace against the band: slow, steady (green), rushed (hatched). Each line is scored per segment for how close you stay and how steady your pace is; rushing past the band blows the line out (it spreads), lifting or stalling and starting again leaves a join, and lingering digs in (trauma). The liner matters: each needle lays its own weight and the one either side ("lays 5–9RL"); a line the liner can't lay costs quality, and the chips say which one to switch to.
- **Shading and colour:** hold and sweep inside a shading zone, or swipe back and forth in a colour zone (colour only packs while you keep reversing). The meter fills to a line; past the hatching you are overworking the skin and it goes red. Next zone moves on.
- **The client:** pain (placement, their tolerance, the machine and voltage, time in the chair, any trauma) and fatigue rise as you work; both meters have notches, a pattern and an icon, and a warning triangle near the top. Break (less each time), Numb (numbing cream, 250 ₽ in 2000 money, takes the edge off for two hours) and Chat (helps with a talkative client, wears on a quiet one). Either meter full ends the sitting. Handedness (Settings › Session) puts these buttons on your free hand's side.
- **Sittings and pieces:** a big piece is split into sittings by hours, mostly linework first. A booked sitting pays its share of the balance and books the next one; progress rides on the booking. Finish everything early and the balance is paid then. If pain or fatigue stops the last sitting, the client comes back the next week to finish, and pays then. The last sitting makes the piece, with its outputs (lines, depth, saturation, trauma, sittings, blowouts, joins), the ink and machine, and the heal due two weeks later (shown in PR 5). Sample walk-ins pay you 35% of the price (the shop keeps the rest) and can sit again straight away. Practice pays nothing and makes no piece. Every sitting gives XP in linework, shading, colour, speed and the style.
- **A sitting survives a reload:** it's saved at every finger lift and comes back exactly where it was. Career › Next waits while a booked sitting is in the chair.
- **The kit (Studio tab):** a summary (steady band, the line weights the tray lays, shading speed, skin trauma, ink fade) and four views: Machine (used coil, coil, rotary, wireless pen from 2014), Needles (liners 1RL to 14RL and mags; up to 5 on the tray), Ink (budget, standard, premium), Tune (voltage and stroke sliders). Every option shows ▲/▼ deltas against what you have. A new career starts with 3,000 ₽ and the starter kit: a used coil, 3RL, 7RL and a 7 mag, budget ink.
- **Sound:** a machine buzz under your finger whose pitch follows your pace and whose tone follows the machine (the used coil rattles, the pen hums); it stutters when you rush. Off with Settings › Sound.
- **Assist** (Settings › Session) slows the marker and widens what counts as steady and on the line.

## 0.1.3.0: clients, the consult and the week

- **Career tab, Next:** the week clock. Next runs the weeks forward until someone is due in the chair, a walk-in arrives, a piece heals or an event fires, never more than 4 weeks at a time, and shows one line ("Weeks 22 to 25, 2004: …") with the week's log under it: walk-ins who left, returns, referrals, bookings, sittings moved. If anyone is waiting this week the first tap warns ("Skip them? Tap again"): walk-ins leave, sittings move to next week. The career stops at week 52 of 2030.
- **Clients tab, This week:** the chair hours booked against the week's hours and the waiting list; walk-ins at the door (they leave at the end of the week); booked consults (clients coming back for a touch-up, an add-on, a sleeve or a cover-up, always naming their earlier piece and its year, and people sent by a client); the sittings due; what's coming up.
- **The consult:** a short chat (3 to 5 exchanges) with the client's brief, written in Mostograd (the Old Bridge, Lodochnaya Embankment, Kanatnaya Street, the port, the White Nights). Every client is an adult and the consult says "ID checked, 18+". Questions reveal hidden must-haves, dealbreakers, pain tolerance, sun, budget and whether they cover up for work (more often with a better consult skill and a trusting, talkative client). A bad idea (tiny script, a portrait on a hand, fine line on a finger) can be talked out, gently or bluntly: win and the plan changes and they trust you more; lose and they may walk. It ends with a quote (fair, high or low): price, deposit and sittings from the design model; they accept or walk. The deposit goes straight into your cash. Choices replay the same way if you leave and come back.
- **Designing for a booking:** "Design it" opens the board for that client with their body shape, skin tone, placement and style, and a bar with what they asked for and what you know. The board scores fit against what the consult found out, not what's still hidden. "Save to booking" puts the design on the sitting.
- **Sittings:** (replaced by the session in 0.1.4.0) "Quick session" worked a sitting out from your skills. Each sitting pays its share of the balance; the last one makes the piece, named the way the client would say it ("the rose on Darya's left forearm").
- **Client list:** everyone you've consulted, newest first, with filters (All, Regulars, Booked, Walked) and a name search. A client's card shows what you know, a loyalty bar, their pieces as they look today (re-rendered from the design and aged by the model) and their history.
- **Walk-ins** depend on the stage (an apprentice sees one now and then), the season and reputation; returns come from your pieces as they age and from loyalty; referrals arrive a few weeks after a good heal.

## 0.1.2.0: the design board

- **Design tab, three views:** Body, Board and Flash, on a segmented control at the top.
- **Body:** an original front/back drawing of the neutral mannequin (smooth capsule limbs, no face, crop top and shorts so the ribs show), in `app/bodymap.js`. Every one of the 26 placements is a tappable (and keyboard-focusable) zone; picking one shows its room (wrap × length for the body shape), pain, how much it shows and its sun. A body-shape switch (Broad, Slight, Average) reshapes the figure and the wrap widths, and a skin-tone slider tints it; both feed scoring. A list picker does the same for anyone who prefers it.
- **Walk-ins:** four sample clients (Darya, Bogdan, Sasha, Ilya) to score against until real clients arrive in PR 3. Picking one sets the body shape and skin tone.
- **Board:** cream with black ink in both themes. The placement's box at its true aspect in millimetres, with a mm ruler, the flow lines, the joint creases and the piece's footprint (W × H mm). Elements come from a drawer in the sheet, filtered by style, family and tag; locked elements are greyed with what unlocks them, and "Show all (sandbox)" opens everything for testing (not saved). Drag to move, two fingers to pinch-scale and rotate, the corner handle for mouse users, and a toolbar: size, rotate ±15° (90° steps from the flow with snap on), line weight (1 to 14RL), fill, colour swatches from the style's palette (greyed, hatched and labelled "faint" or "won't read" when `SKIN_CONTRAST` says so on this skin tone), duplicate, bring forward, delete. Snap to flow turns an element's long axis along the limb's flow where it sits. Lines are drawn at their true width, so a 1RL line on a big piece is a hairline.
- **Capsule:** docked at the bottom of the board: readability, flow, fit, hours, visits (sessions) and price in rubles, each with a ▲/▼ delta from before the last change (colour and shape). Under it, the piece now and at 5, 10 and 20 years, blurred, faded and with the lines spread by `agePrediction`, with readability under each. The chevron hides the aging row (hidden by default on short phones).
- **Stress report:** every `stress` line with a severity icon (octagon serious, triangle warning, circle note), plus balance, coherence and overall.
- **Flash wall:** 12 pre-composed designs (`FLASH`, `sim/flash.js`) that take 20% fewer hours (`FLASH_DISCOUNT`, `design.flash`). Using one loads it on the current spot (shrunk to fit if needed). Adding or removing elements makes it a custom piece again. "Save to my flash" keeps your own designs (up to 40) as your flash, with the same discount.
- **Undo/redo:** buttons, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y). On desktop, Tab reaches each element; arrows nudge (Shift for 5 mm), + and − size, [ and ] turn, Delete removes, Escape deselects.
- **Saving:** the working design, body shape, skin tone and walk-in (`S.board`) and your flash (`S.flash`) save with the game and ride in backup codes; designs save as parameters, never pictures.

## 0.1.1.0: the design sim (text only)

- **Element library:** 135 curated elements (motifs, lettering, frames, fills, textures) across 9 styles; 105 drawn for Ink, 30 from game-icons.net (CC BY 3.0, `art/CREDITS.md`). Lettering is six pre-drawn script words, two roman-numeral dates and banners; the game never renders typed text as art.
- **Placements:** 26 zones on arms, shoulders, hands, legs, feet, back, upper chest, ribs/side and neck, each with wrap and length, pain, visibility, sun, joint creases, flow lines and a UV island. Three body shapes scale the wrap.
- **Scoring:** readability (size vs each element's minimum, density, line weight, skin contrast), flow, balance, coherence, fit to the client, a total, hours, sessions and a price in rubles, and a plain-English stress report.
- **Aging:** blur, fade, spread and today's readability from line weight, depth, colours, placement wear, style, ink tier, skin tone, sun and heal grade.
- **Design tab:** a sample piece and the raw score object, with placement, size, colour and client pickers (replaced by the board in 0.1.2.0).
- **Money:** rubles (₽), with a ruble-like `PRICE_INDEX` from 2000 to 2030.
